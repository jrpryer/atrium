::: page prose | The Count
It was the Keeper who started this, and I want that on the record. They came into the Library one evening with that particular look and said, "Mab. The holes in Old Stoic. Pilgrims. Tithe-nails, gold beaten round iron, every one." And I said, "I knew that," which was not true, and went straight out with the ladder.

There are forty-one holes in Old Stoic. All square, all at about the height of a raised hand, which is to say a pilgrim's hand, which is to say I was right all these years to find them interesting.

Ada says I counted one twice. I say Ada is welcome to come out and count them herself. Ada says she does not climb ladders for gossip. I said it is not gossip, it is a census, and a census is history. She went quiet, which in Ada is a full apology.

::: page list | Rumours, Ranked
RUMOURS ABOUT THE NAILS, RANKED BY HOW MUCH I LIKE THEM

1. Each nail held a pilgrim's promise. (Established.
   The Keeper has evidence, which is more than most
   of my sources can say.)
2. A pilgrim who drove a crooked nail had to walk
   the whole Stair again. (The baker's grandmother.)
3. On still nights the tree hums where the gold was.
   (I have listened. It might. It might be my knees.)
4. Old Stoic swallowed the nails. (Tamsin. She was
   joking. I think she was joking.)
5. Hole twenty-six is not a nail-hole at all, but a
   woodpecker with ambitions. (Mine. I stand by it.)
6. Ada drove one in herself, as a girl, for a wish.
   (Nobody says this. I have just started it.)

Further rumours welcome. Leave them at the desk.
Ada will pretend not to read them.
