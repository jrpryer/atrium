::: page prose | The Salamander Egg
I begin with the egg, because that is where I began, and where I first said sorry.

A salamander's egg is smooth and oval, and it is cold: cold as a river stone, and every bit as patient. You may carry one in your pocket for a month and believe you have picked up a pebble. Many people have. I have.

It wakes only when it is laid in sunlit water. There it warms, slowly, from the inside out. Then it begins to glow, a soft light through the shell like a coal seen through a curtain. At last it hatches, and you have a salamander: small, indignant, and looking for someone to blame.

Snails gather on the eggs while they warm. I cannot say whether they come for the heat or for the company, but a ring of snails on a stone in the shallows is the surest sign I know that the stone is not a stone.

::: page list | Other Small Fire-Beasts, With Apologies
THE EMBER-MOTH
  Lives in lamp-chimneys. Eats soot. Is mistaken for soot.
  Apology: "I am sorry I cleaned your house."

THE HEARTH-CRICKET
  Sings only when the kettle is nearly on the boil.
  Apology: "I am sorry I took the kettle off."

THE CINDER-NEWT
  A salamander's cousin. Sulks in a cold grate for weeks.
  Apology: a warm brick, offered without comment.

THE SPARK-WREN
  Not a bird. Not a spark. Nobody is certain.
  Apology: "I am sorry I asked."

THE PIPE-WORM
  Curls up in old pipes and is perfectly content there.
  Apology: chiefly to the owner of the pipe.
