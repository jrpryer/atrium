::: page prose | How the Tiers Are Reckoned
The Crown reckons the city from the top down, as the Crown reckons everything. The first tier is the Crown itself. The Gallery is the second, the Ribs the third, the Hem the fourth. The Atrium, at the canyon floor, is counted fifth when it is counted at all, which is not often. The High Hallows above the Crown are not counted, being, in the view of this office, "not quite the city, and in any case very cold".

A tier is measured in stairs, a stair being one step of the Long Stair as cut, not as climbed. A clerk who climbs two at a time does not halve the distance, only the clerk's dignity. Stairs are counted from the top step of one tier to the top step of the next. Disputes about landings are to be settled by the charter office, in writing, in triplicate, after lunch.

The official counts follow. Please do not recount them. Every year somebody recounts them, every year they get a different number, and every year I have to write a letter.
