::: page prose | The Two Breaths of Water
Sit down. No, properly. A pool will tell you nothing while you are hopping about on the bank.

Every pool breathes two ways. The first breath runs between Light and Dark: sun on the surface at one end, the cool shade under the roots at the other. The second runs between Hot and Cold: a pool warmed by stones that held the afternoon, or one fed straight from a cold spring. We count each breath in five steps either way, five toward Light and five toward Dark, five toward Hot and five toward Cold, with an even, untroubled nothing in the middle.

Every creature has a place on those two breaths where it will survive, and a smaller place inside that one where it will thrive. Surviving is eating and keeping out of trouble. Thriving is colour, appetite, and a certain swagger. Learn the difference; it is most of the craft. A creature that is only surviving is asking you, politely, to move something.

::: page prose | Feeding, Cleansing, and Sulking
Feed little and often. Scatter a pinch, then watch. Whatever is still drifting after a slow count of twenty was more than they wanted, so skim it off and give less tomorrow.

A pool needs cleansing, too. Green creeps up the walls, silt settles on the bottom, and the water forgets itself and goes the colour of weak tea. Skim it, rinse it, let the snails see to the walls, and the pool remembers what it is.

Now the thing the restless always want to know. Nothing dies in a Stillkeeper's pool. When creatures are neglected (and you will neglect them; everyone does) they sulk. They tuck themselves into the reeds, let their colours go dull, turn their backs on you, and refuse to breed until you have put things right. It is very effective. Put things right, sit a while, and one by one they drift out again, pretending nothing happened.

::: page prose | On Breeding, and Its Surprises
Like breeds like. Two Grey Guppies make Grey Guppies, and you should be pleased with them.

The surprises are where the joy is, so here are the ones I trust.

Heat brings out the blaze. Spotted Spinners bred in very hot water will sometimes hatch a Blazing Glory among their spotted young.

A Spinner mother and a Glory father make a Sun-Spotted child. The other way round, they won't. Do not ask me why; ask the fish. Remember it as the novices do: spots from the mother, sun from the father.

Grey Guppies bred in the deepest shade hatch Shadow Guppies, as though the dark had soaked into them.

Motes are not bred at all, so stop introducing them to one another. They spawn from minerals and auras laid in the spawning pond, and they come in their own time, as most good things do.
