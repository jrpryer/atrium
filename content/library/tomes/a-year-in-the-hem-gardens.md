::: page prose | Spring, and the Clay
Everything in the Hem comes home red-footed, and so will you. Accept it now. The clay of our tier is a deep rust red, fine as flour when it is dry and faithful as a dog when it is wet, and it gets on boots, on knees, on cats, on bread, and somehow on the ceiling. My mother said it was the city's way of signing its work.

It is also the best growing ground in Stairfall, which is why we forgive it. In early spring, when the ledges stop dripping ice, turn your beds with a fork, never a spade; a spade comes up wearing the whole bed. Sow peas along the south wall, where the cliff gives back the afternoon warmth. Set out beans after the first warm rain. Mulch everything with stove ash and old straw, and say something kind to it.

Then wash your hands. It won't help, but it is traditional.

::: page poetry | Midsummer
Midsummer, and the beans have climbed
the trellis and the gate;
the cat has claimed the marrow bed,
the peas are running late.

The washing on the line is pink,
and pink it means to stay;
I scrubbed it till my knuckles sang
and called it 'dawn' all day.

The swifts go screaming down the lane,
the bees go home to sup,
and every boot beside the door
is red from the soles up.

So plant me where the clay is deep
and water me at nine,
and I'll come up the way we do:
red-rooted, and just fine.

::: page prose | Autumn, Winter, and Leeks
Autumn in the Hem is the season of jars. Beans dry on strings across the kitchen, onions hang in plaits by the door, and the last green tomatoes ripen on the sill in a row like a small choir. Pull the marrows before the first frost, or the frost will pull them for you, softly, into mush.

Clear the beds and leave the clay rough; the cold breaks it better than any fork. Throw the spent stalks on the heap with a shovel of ash from the stove. By spring the heap is black and sweet and the worms are fat as thumbs.

In winter there is nothing to do but read seed lists and argue with the neighbours about leeks. This is the most important work of the year. A gardener who has not argued about leeks by midwinter will sow them too close, and deserve it.

Keep a rag by the door. Keep two.
