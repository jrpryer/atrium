::: page prose | True Amber
Amber is not a stone, whatever the Gallery gem-sellers call it while they are pricing it. It is the resin of old trees, wept out through the bark ages ago and turned to stone by patience. Remember that, and you are already harder to cheat.

Here is how to know it. True amber is light as pumice: a piece the size of a hen's egg weighs hardly more than the shell. Close it in your hand and it warms quickly, almost at once, as if it had been waiting for you. Bring it near a candle flame (near, mind, not into) and it gives off a faint smell of resin, like pine sap on a hot afternoon. Glass does none of these things.

The finest pieces carry a passenger: a gnat, a beetle, a tiny moth, asleep inside since long before the city cut its first stair. Do not pay extra for the insect's expression. They all look surprised.

::: page prose | The Imitations
Now the rogues' gallery, which in the Gallery is a short walk.

Copal is young resin, not yet given its centuries. It looks well enough and is honestly light, but it goes tacky under a warm thumb, and a drop of spirit leaves a dull print on it. It is amber that has not finished its homework.

Glass is the cheapest lie and the easiest caught. It is cold, it stays cold, and it sits heavy in the palm. Any insect inside it was put there by a person with tweezers and no shame.

Ember-glass is the clever one: a honey-tinted glass from the back-street furnaces, sometimes smeared with a little real sap to fool your nose. Weigh it. Ember-glass is always too heavy for its size, and its smell burns off in a single pass of the flame, while true amber smells of resin every time.

If a seller will not let you hold the piece, buy the seller a cup of tea instead, and keep your money.
