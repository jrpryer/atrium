::: page prose | The Evening, in Order
For the record, and because my sister's version is already going round the Hem with embellishments:

The lamps of the Atrium were relit on an evening in the year six hundred and forty, a little after Old Stoic's shadow reached the near pool. [Correction: the far pool. Mab had moved the bench.] The sky was clear. The falls were their usual thin turquoise. Eleven people were present, not counting the Keeper, two cats, and a Stillkeeper who arrived late and behaved as though he had been there all along.

The Keeper lit the first lamp, by the Library door. It took on the second try. [Correction: the first try. The second try was Mab, lighting her pipe from it.] Then the Apothecary, then the Pools, then the Sanctum, in that order, which is the order of the charter and therefore the correct one.

How the light was got is the Keeper's to tell. I have reserved them a shelf.

::: page prose | Afterwards
Afterwards there was tea, and Tamsin brought a cake which she said was an experiment and which was, in fact, a cake. Nobody made a speech. Mab tried, and I passed her the cake.

I will record one thing that is not a fact, because I am told a history ought to have one. When the fourth lamp took, the light ran out across the water and up the bark of the tree, and for a moment every face was lit from below, the way faces were lit when I was very small and the lamplighter still came down each summer. I had forgotten that I had forgotten it.

The lamps have burned every night since. They are in oil. I check. The Keeper checks too, though they think I do not notice them doing it.

Corrected twice, for accuracy. The third correction, which Mab requested, is declined.
