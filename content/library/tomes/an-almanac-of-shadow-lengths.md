::: page prose | Why a Tree
There are no clocks in the Atrium that anyone has wound in my lifetime, and the Crown's bells do not carry this far down. By the time they reach the canyon floor they have worn themselves out into a sort of hum. So we tell the time the old way, by Old Stoic.

The sun comes late to the bottom of the city and leaves early, and while it is here the great tree's shadow moves across the courtyard like the hand of a slow and dignified clock. We measure it in two units only. A hand's width is the shortest shadow of the day, near noon, when the tree stands almost on its own dark. A pool's length is the long stride of morning and afternoon: as far as from one end of a pool to the other.

All other units are vanity. The table overleaf is for the summer half of the year. In winter, add a pool to everything and forgive yourself.

On cloudy days, guess. On rainy days, nobody minds
what time it is.
