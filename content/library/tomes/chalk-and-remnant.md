::: page prose | The Circle
You have read the first book or you have not. It does not matter. Few have.

A Circle is drawn in chalk, on a floor that will not be swept. It need not be large, but it must be closed. Most who own one take it for a pattern in the floor and walk across it for years. The Circle does not mind.

Into it you lay the remnant: what is left behind when a thing has been broken, burned, ground, or given away into something else. The shards of a jug. The stub of a candle. The husk of a seed. Step out. Wait. The Circle remembers the whole thing better than you do, and it makes the thing whole again.

Above everything else in this book, remember this: matter is reclaimable; knowledge is permanent. What you learned from the thing while it was broken, you keep. The Circle gives you back the jug. It never takes back what the jug taught you.

::: page list | What Goes Quiet, and What Stays
THE TWO RULES

  I.  What is made FROM a thing goes quiet
      when the thing is made whole.

  II. What the thing only TOUCHED stays touched.

EXAMPLES, FOR THE SLOW

  Remake the honeycomb, and the candle poured from
  its wax goes cold and will not light again.
  The room that candle lit was still lit.

  Remake the bell from its bronze, and the spoons
  cast from that bronze go dull and dumb.
  The town that woke to its ringing stays awake.

  Remake the apple, and the cider pressed from it
  goes flat as water. The evening it warmed
  stays warmed.

Draw the Circle when you need the thing itself.
It gives the thing back gladly, and asks only
that what was made from it lie quiet again.
