::: page prose | The Stair and the Token
The Long Stair begins at the shrines of the High Hallows and ends at the bottom of the world, and you will feel every step of it in your knees. Pack light. You will want a stick, good socks, bread, and your token.

Before you set out, make your vow-token. Take a leaf of the Lantern Birch, which grows here and nowhere else, and seal it in clear, hard shrine-wax, so that it keeps its silver for as long as you need it to. Beneath the wax, in brown ink, write your vow. Keep it short; the longer the vow, the longer the walk. Hang the token on a cord and wear it at your breast all the way down the Stair.

When the vow is kept, the token comes back to the shrine, and the wax is melted for another. So if ever you find a token lying loose, do not pocket it for luck. A token found is a vow still open.

::: page prose | The Waystation Trees
All down the Stair stand the waystation trees, where a pilgrim may sit, eat the bread, and take off the socks for a moment, though not for long, for the sake of everyone nearby. Each tier has one, or had one; some have been cut for timber and some have simply been built around. Greet each as you pass. They have heard more pilgrims' grumbling than any priest.

The last of them stands at the very bottom, on the canyon floor beneath the falls: the great tree they call Old Stoic. It leans out over the pools as though it were listening for something. When you reach it, you have reached the end of the Stair, and you may sit down properly.

Rest there. Drink from the pools. Look up at the whole city stacked above you and think how far you have come. Then, when you are ready, turn round. The Stair goes up as well as down, and someone at the top is waiting to hear how it went.

::: page poetry | A Leave-Taking
Shrine of the snow and spring of the cold,
keep what I leave you, and keep what I hold;
I go down the Stair with my vow at my breast,
and I will not sit down till I've earned my rest.

Birch of the Hallows, silver and pale,
light me the way if my lantern should fail;
tree at the bottom, old in the spray,
hold me a while, then send me away.

Stone of the steps, be kind to my feet,
bread in my pocket, stay dry and stay sweet;
and if I come home with my vow come true,
I'll climb the last step and tell it to you.
