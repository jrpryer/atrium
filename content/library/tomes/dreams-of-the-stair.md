::: page poetry | The Stair, Dreamed
I dream the Stair again tonight,
the long one, from the snow,
and every step I take is down
and every step is slow.

My knees say sit. My heart says on.
The lanterns say not far.
And under all of it the falls
keep saying what they are.

I pass the shrines. I pass the bread
the birds have half-way eaten.
I pass a fellow on a landing
who swears that he is beaten.

I tell him rest, then off you go;
the dream won't let me choose.
I wake at six with aching knees
and no stairs in my shoes.

::: page poetry | The Falls, Dreamed
In the dream the falls are not lazy.
They come down all at once,
loud as a market, bright as a knife,
and I stand at the bottom with my mouth open
like a fish.

My grandmother says she saw them glitter
when she was small.
Everyone's grandmother says so.
I think the whole city is dreaming
the same dream, one tier at a time.

In the morning they are thin again,
a turquoise thread,
patient, in no hurry at all,
falling the way a cat sits down.

I go to the window and tell them, kindly,
take your time.
They do.
