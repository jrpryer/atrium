::: page prose | On Pond Flakes
Most of what lives in our pools eats flakes, and most of what goes wrong in our pools begins with too many of them.

Pond flakes are nothing grand: green scraped from the pool walls, dried thin on a warm slate, and crumbled between the fingers. Sister Wend dries hers in the sun. I dry mine on the warm stones beside the falls, which she says is cheating. Both work.

Scatter a pinch on the water and count slowly to twenty. Whatever is gone was wanted. Whatever is still floating was not, and should be skimmed out before it sinks. Small mouths are honest. They will not eat to be polite.

The Grey Guppies come up first, every time. The snails will not come up at all, but do not worry about them. They are busy eating the walls.

::: page recipe | Reed Meal
REED MEAL
For fry, young snails, and anything off its food.

You will want:
  a handful of reed, dried until it snaps
  a good pinch of loam, dry and crumbly

Method:
  Break the reed into short lengths.
  Put the reed and the loam together in the mortar.
  Grind slowly, until it is a soft brown meal
  and you cannot tell which was which.
  Sift out any long threads and grind them again.

To serve:
  A fingertip at a time, on still water.
  Keeps a month in a stoppered jar.

With love from the pools,
Br. A.
