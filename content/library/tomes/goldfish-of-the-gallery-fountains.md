::: page prose | Who's Who in the Fountains
The Gallery keeps seven public fountains and, at the last count, rather more than three hundred goldfish, and I am delighted to say that I know the best of them personally.

The Spotted Spinner is the darling of the lower basins: a round little goldfish dusted with dark spots, who cannot pass a patch of sunlight without turning a circle in it. A shoal of Spinners at noon looks like confetti being stirred with a spoon.

The Blazing Glory is another matter entirely. Red-gold from nose to tail, with fins like a lit taper and a temper to match, the Glory keeps to the warmest water it can find and makes a great show of living there. Every fountain wants one. Few fountains deserve one.

Mixed company is common and generally delightful, though I am told one must be careful about who is introduced to whom.

::: page prose | The Glassworks Basin
Old Mister Pruett has kept the little fountain behind the glassworks for thirty years, and for twenty-nine of them it held nothing but Spotted Spinners, which suited him perfectly.

Then came the summer the furnaces ran day and night. The basin sits hard against the furnace wall, and that year its water grew so warm that Mister Pruett took to warming his tea in it.

In the autumn his Spinners hatched as usual, round and spotted and ordinary, and among them, bold as a struck match, was one small Blazing Glory.

"Heat brings out the blaze," he told me, as though he had known it all along. He had not. He had been warming his tea in the fountain. But the Glory grew up very handsome, and Mister Pruett now tells everyone that his basin is the warmest address in the Gallery, which, to be fair, it is.
