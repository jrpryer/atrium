::: page prose | Gardens a Doorstep Wide
In the Ribs we garden the way we live: sideways, and close to the wall. Every door in the cistern-quarters opens onto a ledge, and every ledge worth the name has a herb box on it: a crate, a drawer, a cracked pot, or, in my neighbour's case, his late father's boots.

The ledges are narrow but warm. The rock holds the day's heat and gives it back at evening, and the damp breathing up out of the cisterns keeps the soil from drying. Thyme loves it. Sage puts up with it. Mint, given half a chance, will take the ledge, the door, and the lease. Plant mint in a pot inside another pot, and watch it like a cousin.

Water at dusk with a jug, not a can, so you don't wash your neighbour's chives into the lane below. Pick from the top. Pick often. A herb wants to be eaten, and the more you take, the more it gives.

::: page recipe | Ledge Soup, for Divers Coming Up Cold
For anyone who has come up out of the cisterns blue at
the lips, or who simply wants soup. Serves four, or two
divers.

You will want:
  a good knob of fat
  two onions, chopped
  a fist of barley
  a jug of stock, or water and a bone
  a handful of thyme, stripped from the stalk
  six sage leaves, torn
  a pinch of mint, at the end and not before
  salt, and pepper if the Gallery has been kind

Soften the onions in the fat, slowly, until they smell
like supper. Add the barley and stir until it shines.
Pour in the stock, add the thyme and sage, and let it
murmur on a low fire for as long as it takes to tell a
long story. Season. Tear in the mint just before serving,
so it stays bright.

Serve in a deep bowl, with bread. Hand it to the diver
before you ask how the intakes were. They will tell you
anyway.
