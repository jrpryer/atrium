::: page letter | From a Gem-Seller
To Whom It Should Have Concerned Long Ago, from Mistress Hepzibah Rook, gem-seller, third stall from the fountain.

Sirs,

I write again about the glassworks. Since they took the yard behind my stall, my candles droop by noon and my customers go pink about the ears. I have no objection to glass. I have every objection to being baked.

Further, their youngest apprentice has been selling green bottle-glass by the fountain as "river emerald", at a price I would blush to ask for a real one. I spoke to him. He said everybody does it. Everybody does not. I do not.

While I have your ear: the cheese-seller's dog was found asleep again in the Assayers' Exchange, on the great scales, and was weighed, and the weight was entered in the ledger. I feel somebody ought to know.

Yours, at a rising temperature,
H. Rook

::: page letter | From a Botanist
Dear Neighbours,

I am a botanist. I keep forty herbarium drawers above the flower market, and I should like, only once, to open one without finding it full of soot.

The glassworks smoke comes in under the shutters. My ferns, which I pressed with great care in the year of my marriage, smell of a chimney. My mosses smell of a chimney. My husband says that I smell of a chimney, and he is not wrong, and he is not helping.

I should also like a word with the young people who come to my door with a dried flower in a handkerchief and ask what it is worth. A pressed flower is not worth anything. It is worth knowing. That is the whole point of it. If you want money, go and bother the gem-sellers. They have it coming.

With affection for everybody except the glassworks,
Dr. Aurelie Fenn, botanist (retired, mostly)
