::: page prose | What the Reliquaries Keep
People imagine a Stillkeeper's reliquary stuffed with saints' knucklebones and old teeth. I am sorry to disappoint them. Our reliquaries keep keys to things that should stay closed.

That is all, and it is a great deal. Each key is small, small enough to lose in a glove, and each is finely warded: its bit cut into a little maze of notches and steps, so fine that no other key in Stairfall would turn the same lock. They are not keys to doors anyone ought to open. They are kept so that certain doors stay shut, and kept by us, who have no wish to open them, rather than by anyone who might be tempted.

A key in a reliquary is a promise not to use it. Once a year we take each one out, polish it, and put it back. That is the whole of the ceremony, and it is enough.

::: page prose | The Balance
In the Sanctum, the Keeper's own lodge, stands the Balance: an old riddle-lock of brass made in the shape of a scale, set over a sealed reliquary by Stillkeepers whose names we did not keep. Cut into its beam are the only words it has ever said:

"Lay upon me a thing of shadow and of flame, and I will open."

It means what it says, and it is particular. Shadow alone will not move it; I once watched a novice set a bowl of ink in its pan and wait the whole of an afternoon. Fire alone will not move it either; a lit lamp only warms the brass and makes the Keeper anxious about the floor.

I have never seen it open, and I am content with that. Some locks are made for opening. Some are made for teaching patience to people who would very much like to open them.
