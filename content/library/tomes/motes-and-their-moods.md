::: page prose | Where Motes Come From
Motes are not bred. I say this first because every novice tries it anyway, holding two sun-motes together in cupped hands and waiting hopefully. Nothing happens. The motes are embarrassed for them.

A mote spawns. Lay a mineral or an aura in the spawning pond and leave it be, and in its own good time the pond will give up a mote, the way a hearth gives up a spark. What you lay in decides what comes out. A bright thing calls up sun-motes, little points of warm light that hang just under the surface. A cold thing calls up frost-motes, pale and sharp, that drift near the bottom and leave a faint chill on your fingers.

I will not pretend to understand it. The pond understands it. My task, and now yours, is to keep the pond clean and be patient.

::: page prose | Their Moods
Sun-motes are cheerful, nosy, and vain. They crowd toward anyone carrying a lamp, and they sulk, dimming almost to nothing, if their pool is left too long in shade. Praise them and they brighten. I have seen it happen.

Frost-motes are cooler in every sense. They prefer their own company and the cold end of a pool, and they will drift away, offended, if the water warms. They are not unkind. They are simply particular, like certain aunts.

Neither kind will be hurried, scolded, or argued with; they are too small for argument and they know it. A dim mote is not failing. It is telling you, in the only way it can, that the water is wrong for it. Mend the water and wait. A sulking mote comes back to itself the way a cold room warms: slowly, and without thanking you.
