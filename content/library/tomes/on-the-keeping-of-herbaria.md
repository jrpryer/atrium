::: page prose | The Proper Method
A specimen is a letter to the future, saying: this plant grew in this place on this day. Write the letter properly or do not write it at all.

The method is simple and admits no improvement. Lay the specimen flat between two sheets of clean, dry paper, arranged as it grew, leaves open, nothing folded. Press it under a board and weights, and change the papers daily for a week. When it is dry, mount it on a stiff sheet and label it in a clear hand with the place it was gathered and the date. A specimen without its place and date is a pretty thing and nothing more. I have thrown out whole drawers of pretty things.

A specimen is never sealed in wax. I am asked this every season. Wax yellows within a few years, clouds, and spoils the specimen beneath it beyond any use. Nor is a specimen ever strung on a cord or worn about the person, like a keepsake. A herbarium sheet lives flat, in a drawer, in the dark, and is grateful.

::: page prose | Drawers, Beetles, and Colleagues
A herbarium is a cabinet of shallow drawers, and every drawer is a small country with its own customs. Mine run by family, then by tier, then by year gathered, and I will not discuss why with anyone who has not read the previous page.

The enemies of a herbarium are three: damp, beetles, and colleagues. Damp you defeat with a dry room and a stove kept low. Beetles you defeat with camphor and vigilance. Colleagues cannot be defeated, only managed. They borrow sheets and return them to the wrong drawer. They open a drawer, sneeze, and close it again. One of them, whom I will not name except to say that he keeps a stall near the glassworks, once used a pressed fern as a bookmark.

I have forgiven him. I have also changed the lock.
