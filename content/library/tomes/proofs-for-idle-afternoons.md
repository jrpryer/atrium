::: page prose | The Nine Buttons
Mab has sewn a button onto my winter coat. It is a perfectly good button, except that she used enough thread to moor a boat, and it is now heavier than its fellows. I have taken all nine buttons off the coat (I will explain this to Mab later) and mixed them up, and they look alike.

The puzzle. With a pair of balance scales, borrowed from the Apothecary, and only two weighings, find the heavy button.

Answer. Divide the buttons into three heaps of three. Put one heap in each pan. If one pan sinks, the heavy button is in that heap; if they balance, it is in the heap left on the table. Now take the guilty heap and put one button in each pan. If one sinks, there it is. If they balance, it is the one still in your hand.

Two weighings. I did it in none, by asking Mab.

::: page prose | Sisters on a Stair
Mab and I set out together up a stair of one hundred steps. I climb one step with every breath, steadily, and do not stop. Mab climbs two steps with every breath, which she calls "getting on with it", but at every twentieth step she stops for ten breaths to talk to whoever is there. There is always somebody there.

The puzzle. Which of us reaches the top first, and by how many breaths?

Answer. I take one hundred breaths, one for each step. Mab climbs the hundred steps in fifty breaths, and she stops at the twentieth, fortieth, sixtieth and eightieth. She does not stop at the hundredth, because by then she has arrived. Four stops of ten breaths is forty breaths. Fifty and forty is ninety.

Mab arrives first, by ten breaths, and spends them telling me so. I have checked this four times. It is correct. I dislike it very much.
