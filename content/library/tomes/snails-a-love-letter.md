::: page letter | Parts One to Four
My dear Yellow-back,

I. You are not quick. I want you to know I have never once held it against you.

II. You keep the pools. While the rest of us stand about talking of clear water, you go round the walls in the night and graze off every speck of green, slowly, thoroughly, without being asked. In the morning the water is clear and you are sitting in a corner looking as though you had nothing to do with it.

III. You glow. Not much; only a little, and only in warm light, a faint lamp-yellow along the ridge of your shell. I have sat up whole evenings to watch it come and go.

IV. You like your water warm and bright, and you are not shy about saying so. Put you somewhere cold and dark and you simply close your door and wait for me to come to my senses.

::: page poetry | Parts Five to Seven
V.
I set you on the pool wall at noon
and found you at dusk an inch along,
and the whole of that inch was clean.

VI.
They ask me what you are good for.
I tell them: the water. The walls.
The long, slow argument against green.
A small warm lamp among the reeds
when the evening is kind enough.

VII.
Stay as long as you like.
Take the slow way round.
I will keep the water warm and bright
and you will keep it clear,
and neither of us will hurry,
which is the whole of love,
or near enough.

Yours, at your own pace,
Pim
