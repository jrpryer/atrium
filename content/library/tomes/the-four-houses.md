::: page prose | The Library and the Apothecary
There are four houses round Old Stoic, and I have lived in one of them for fifty years, so you may take this as gospel or as gossip, whichever suits. My sister will tell you it is gossip. My sister is wrong about a great many things, though she shelves them beautifully.

The Library is ours, mine and Ada's. Ada does the order. I do the knowing what is in the books, which is the harder half, though nobody gives you a ribbon for it.

The Apothecary is Tamsin Pell's now, and was Old Corran Pell's before her. Corran kept one of our books for eleven years and returned it smelling of vinegar. Tamsin, to her great credit, has paid off the vinegar in jam. She starts every apprentice on water, ash, and patience, and every apprentice asks when they will get to the interesting part, and she says, "That was it."

::: page prose | The Pools and the Sanctum
The Spirit Pools were the Stillkeepers' charge, and a quieter, kinder, more maddening order you could not hope to meet. They would sit by the water all afternoon and call it work. Then, about the year six hundred and ten, the pools clouded, and the Stillkeepers went away up the stair, all in one week, and never said a word about it to me, which I have still not forgiven. They owe us four books and an explanation.

The Sanctum is the Keeper's own lodge, with the journal desk, and the Stillkeepers' old Balance that will not open for anybody, and a chalk circle in the floor that every Keeper takes for decoration. I have told them it is not decoration. I do not know what it is, but I know what decoration looks like, and that is not it.

So: who owes whom? Everyone owes the Library. The Library owes nobody. Ada checked.
