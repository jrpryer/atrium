::: page prose | The Rule
There is one rule of shelving in this house, and it is mine: oldest nearest the lamp.

Every shelf in the Library has a lamp end. The book that stands nearest the lamp is the oldest on that shelf, by the year it was written. Beside it stands the next oldest, and beside that the next, year after year, until the newest stands farthest from the light, at the dim end, where it may wait until it has earned better. Every shelf runs the same way, whatever it holds: from the oldest year to the newest.

The reasons are three. Old ink fades, and wants the best light. Old books are the foundations of the new, and foundations go first. And a shelf so ordered is a line of time laid flat: walk along it away from the lamp and you walk forward through the years.

That is the whole of the geometry. It is very simple. Mab has nevertheless found something to say about it.

::: page letter | Mab's Rebuttal
Ada,

I have read your page one, all of it, twice, which is twice more than anyone else will. Here is my rebuttal. I am putting it in your book because you will not let me put it in mine.

Nobody in the history of reading has ever walked into a library and said, "I should like something from SR 455, please." They say, "Have you got the one about the moss?" or "the one with the soup in it". People find books by what is inside them, and by who wrote them, and by whose cousin is in them. Oldest nearest the lamp is very tidy and very cold, like frost on a window.

Also, the oldest books are the dullest, and you have put them in the best light. I rest my case.

Your loving and correct sister,
Mab

(Ada's note: the shelves remain by year. The case may remain rested.)
