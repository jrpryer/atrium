::: page prose | In Defence of Grey
Every fancier at the Gallery fountains will tell you that grey is not a colour. Every fancier at the Gallery fountains is wrong, and I have told them so, at length, at several dinners.

The Grey Guppy is the kindest fish a beginner can keep. It is hardy. It is forgiving. It will put up with water a little too warm or a little too bright, a missed feeding, a clumsy net, and a keeper who talks to it far too much, and it will not hold any of it against you. Neglect it and it sulks for a day or two, then forgives you the moment you come back with a pinch of flakes.

And grey, looked at properly, is not one colour but forty: pewter, rain, river-stone, the underside of a cloud. A shoal of them turning all at once is like a sigh.

::: page prose | Of Shadow Guppies
Now for the part the fanciers do pay attention to.

A pair of Grey Guppies bred in ordinary water will give you more Grey Guppies, and you should be glad of every one. But breed a pair in the deepest shade a pool can hold, down where the light gives up, and some of the young come out dark: velvet-black, with fins like dusk and eyes like wet ink. Those are Shadow Guppies. They are what the shade makes of a grey fish.

Only the deepest shade will do. A shady corner is not enough. A cloudy week is not enough. I have tried a pantry, a cupboard, and once, I am sorry to say, a hat.

A Shadow Guppy is no harder to keep than its grey parents. It simply prefers the dark, and who could blame it.
