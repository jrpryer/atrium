::: page prose | First Watch
The charter asks the Keeper to keep the stillness in good repair. When I first read that, I laughed. Then I spent eleven years learning what it meant.

Stillness wears out like anything else. Boots wear it thin. So do hurry, and worry, and the habit of looking up every time a leaf comes down. It must be mended, and the mending is done by sitting.

Sit by the pools in the early morning. Do not count anything. Do not plan the day. Watch the water until you stop seeing water and begin to see the light in it. That is the first watch. It is not nothing. It only looks like nothing, which is how you know you are doing it properly.

::: page list | Sayings for the Second Watch
1. Doing nothing is not the same as not doing
   anything. Ask any cat.

2. The tree has not moved in all my years here.
   It is not idle. It is busy staying.

3. If you must hurry, hurry slowly. If you
   cannot hurry slowly, sit down.

4. A still pool shows you the sky.
   A stirred pool shows you mud.

5. Nothing in the Atrium is late. Things
   arrive when they arrive.

6. Keep the lamps in oil and the stillness in
   good repair, and much of the rest will keep
   itself.

7. Put the kettle on. This is also a vigil.
