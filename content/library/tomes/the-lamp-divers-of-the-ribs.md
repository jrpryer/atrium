::: page prose | Smoke-Pearls
Ask any lamp-diver about smoke-pearls and watch them go quiet and pleased, like someone remembering a good dinner.

The cisterns of the Ribs are dark all the way down, and a diver works by a sealed lamp. Now and then a lamp gutters at the bottom, and when it does, a cistern-mussel may open and swallow the little flame before it dies. The mussel wraps it in nacre, layer on layer, for years.

What comes of it is a smoke-pearl: black all over, and seamless, with no hole or join that a fingernail can find. It is warm at its heart, though it has sat in cold water for a decade. And it is lighter than it looks; people always expect it to drop into the palm like a stone, and it never does.

Crack one and out pours smoke, grey and slow, that will not disperse. It hangs about the room like a sulking cat. Divers say it is the old flame, still thinking it over.

::: page poetry | The Intake Song
Down, down, by the sealed lamp's glow,
one good breath and the rest below;
clear the grate and pull the weed,
the city drinks what the cisterns feed.
Hold it, hold it, hold it, love,
the Ribs are dark, but the sky's above.

Down, down, where the mussels keep
the guttered lamps in the cold and deep;
lose your flame and never weep,
a mussel will mind it while you sleep.
Hold it, hold it, hold it, love,
the Ribs are dark, but the sky's above.

Up, up, with your chest on fire,
up past the beans on the ledge-wire;
tell the cook to warm the pot,
for the diver's cold and the soup is hot.

::: page prose | Guild Life
The lamp-divers meet on the third evening of each month in a cistern-side hall that smells for ever of wet wool. There are dues, which are paid in lamp-oil, and there are minutes, which are kept by whoever has the driest hands.

A diver's first dive is a shallow one, made holding on to a senior diver's ankle. A diver's hundredth dive is celebrated with a cake in the shape of an intake grate, which is a difficult shape for a cake. Nobody in the guild has ever been able to explain why they keep choosing it.

Divers are proud, superstitious, and kind. They will not whistle in a cistern. They will not lend a lamp that has guttered, in case it guttered for a reason. And they will always, always share a towel.
