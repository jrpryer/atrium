::: page prose | The Lantern-Reed
Everyone who looks at a reed thinks of a basket. Stop it. There are a hundred other things a reed can be, and I mean to set down a good number of them before my thumbs give out.

Begin with the lantern-reed, the best of its kind. It grows at the margins of the Atrium pools in clumps as tall as a child, with a stem that is hollow, light, and stronger than it has any right to be. At dusk its seed-heads hold a faint yellow light, like a lamp seen through a curtain, which is where the name comes from and why the pool margins look so kindly after dark.

Cut it in late summer, when the stem is gold to the knee. Dry it in bundles, heads down, out of the rain. Soak it before working, never after. And if anyone asks what you are making, say "not a basket", and keep walking.

::: page list | Things That Are Not Baskets (Abridged)
- Twine. Beat the soaked reed flat on a stone until it
  splits to threads, then roll the threads on your thigh.
  Reed twine is stronger than it looks. So am I.
- Thatch, for anyone who likes a roof.
- Pens. Cut the end on a slant and split the nib.
- Whistles. Do not give these to children.
- Mats, which are flat, and therefore not baskets.
- Floats for nets, from the hollow stem, stoppered.
- Splints for a cat's leg. The cat will disagree.
- A pouch. Weave it tight, close the seams, and rub it
  hot with wax, inside and out. Done well, it will hold
  water. Done better, a waxed reed pouch will hold even
  smoke.
- Torches, if you must. You must not.
- Hats. Some say a hat is a basket. Some are wrong.
- Ninety-one more, to follow, if my thumbs allow.
