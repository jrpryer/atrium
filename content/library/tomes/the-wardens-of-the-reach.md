::: page prose | The Duties
There are three sluices at the Gallery Reach, and each has its warden. A warden is not an engineer, a clerk, or a gatekeeper, though a warden does a little of all three and complains about each in turn.

The duties are these. To walk the sluice at dawn and at dusk. To clear the grates of leaves, birds' nests, and the occasional hat. To open the sluice when the Crown asks, and close it when the Crown asks, and to keep a log of both in a clear hand. To know the sound of the water as one knows the voice of a friend, and to notice when it changes.

Above all, to hold the key. Each sluice answers to a single heavy key with a silver bit, and the warden keeps the key as a priest keeps a vow: close, always, and with a certain solemnity even at breakfast.

::: page prose | The Oath, and the Hats
On taking office, a warden swears at the sluice-head, with one hand on the key:

"I will hold this key and keep it at the Reach. I will open when I am bidden and close when I am bidden, and not before, and not after. I will not lend it, sell it, wager it, or leave it in a coat."

The keys are never to leave the Reach. I state this plainly because I have been asked. Not for cleaning, not for blessing, not to show a sweetheart in the Gallery. If a key needs mending, the smith comes to the key.

As to hats: the approved hat is broad, waxed, and tied under the chin, for the spray at the Reach never stops and the wind is rude. Feathers are discouraged. One warden wore a hat with a small brass weathervane on top. It was very accurate. It was not approved.
