::: page prose | What Happened, Briefly
In the spring of five hundred and forty-one, Sluice III at the Gallery Reach was closed for repairs. A notice was posted. The notice said the repairs would take a fortnight. The repairs never came.

I was a junior clerk then, and it fell to me to call a meeting of all interested parties. I booked a room in the Crown. I ordered biscuits. I sat with my pen for three hours, and nobody came, and I ate the biscuits, because it seemed a shame to waste them.

That is the whole matter in a sentence: the city barely noticed. Less water came down the falls, and what came was quieter. In the Crown it made no difference to the view. In the Gallery they had other things to complain about. Down in the lower tiers people noticed, but nobody asks the lower tiers. By autumn they were calling it the Year of Quiet Water, fondly, as you might speak of a mild winter.

::: page list | The Minutes, As Kept
MINUTES OF A MEETING CONCERNING SLUICE III
Crown Clerks' Room Four, midsummer, SR 541

Present: J. Tallow, junior clerk (minutes)
Absent: everybody else

1. Sluice III at the Gallery Reach to remain closed
   pending repairs. Repairs to be scheduled.
2. Repairs not scheduled. Clerk to write again.
3. Clerk wrote again. No reply. Noted.
4. The warden of Sluice III "relieved of duty",
   by order. Name not entered in the record.
   The record keeps only the initials: I.F.
5. Clerk asked why. Clerk told it was not a
   clerk's business. Noted, with some feeling.
6. Clerk proposed a small brass plaque.
   Clerk seconded. Carried. Plaque not bought.
7. Biscuits: eaten.
8. Meeting closed. No objections.
