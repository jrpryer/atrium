::: page prose | The Tithe-Nail
Of all the tithes a pilgrim may leave on the way down, the tithe-nail is the oldest and, done properly, the loveliest. It is also the one most often done badly, so attend.

Take a hand-forged iron nail, made by a smith and not bought by the bucket. Take a gold coin. Beat the coin thin around the nail, patiently, until the iron is wholly clothed in gold and the nail shines like a sliver of morning. Then drive it into one of the waystation trees along the descent. Pilgrims have done this for longer than the Stair has had a name, so the tree would hold the promise until they returned.

Use gold blessed at the Hallows springs if you can come by it. Such gold keeps a little of the springs in it: lay it in water, even dull water in a dark cup, and it glows faintly, as if it remembered the light. A tithe of blessed gold is twice a tithe.

::: page list | Other Offerings, and Where to Leave Them
AT THE HIGH SPRINGS
  A cupful of snow, melted in your own two hands.
  Not coins. The springs have no use for coins.

AT THE WAYSIDE SHRINES
  Bread, broken, never whole. If the birds take it,
  that counts. The birds are on the shrine's side.

AT THE LANDINGS OF THE STAIR
  One song, sung once, and not too loudly.

AT THE COLD POOLS
  A ribbon tied to a reed. Untie it on your way home.

NOWHERE, EVER
  Your boots, your bad temper, or your neighbour's goat.
  I have found all three, and returned the goat.
