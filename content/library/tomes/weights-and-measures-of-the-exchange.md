::: page prose | Of Gold and Its Pretenders
Every member of this Exchange swears first upon the scales. The scales do not lie, though the people standing beside them very often do.

Know, then, that gold is the heaviest of all the common metals. A thimble of it outweighs a thimble of anything else a merchant is likely to set on your counter. Silver is lighter, copper lighter again, and iron much lighter still: a piece of iron must be more than twice the size of a piece of gold to balance it on the scales.

From this follows the assayer's first rule. Gilded iron weighs light for its size. Take anything that looks like gold, weigh it, measure it, and if it is light for how big it is, there is iron under that golden skin, however bright the skin.

The cleverer forgers know this, and so they gild lead instead, which is heavy enough to fool a careless hand, though still lighter than true gold. A careful hand is not fooled. Consult the table overleaf, and be careful.
