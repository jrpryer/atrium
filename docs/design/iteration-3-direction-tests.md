# Iteration 3: direction test prompts

*Assembled from [iteration-3-prompts.json](iteration-3-prompts.json) for pasting into any image tool by hand. Each prompt is the subject, then the world, then the direction's medium. Generate each test once per direction (8 × 3 = 24 images), then compare the directions side by side. Choosing one is decision D1 in the [art bible](iteration-3-art-bible.md).*

**Avoid** (put this in the tool's negative-prompt field if it has one; if it doesn't, leave it out, because writing these words into the prompt tends to summon text): text, lettering, letters, words, numbers, captions, signature, watermark, logo, frame or border, cast shadow or contact shadow on isolated subjects, modern objects, plastic, rubber tubing, neon, chrome, sci-fi, photoreal human faces, the player character's body, face, hands, shadow or reflection, cozy game, chibi, kawaii, anime, storybook cottage, gore, horror, gloom, oversaturated colour, airbrushed gradients, lens flare, blurry, duplicated objects, extra limbs, gilt or gold-leaf ornament

**Reference frames for the two courtyard tests.** Paint over a capture of the courtyard from the game's own camera, so the test shows the view players actually get. Use the capture for camera and layout only: its lighting and lamps are iteration 2's. To make the captures, save these lines as `ref.txt`:

```text
new 1310
go hub
pause
ui off
ticks 1980
wait 1.5
dismiss
wait 1.5
shot hub-evening
ticks 1420
wait 1.5
dismiss
wait 1.5
shot hub-noon
```

Then run:

```bash
dotnet run --project src/Atrium.Diorama -- --capture ref.txt --out art/reference/engine
```

## Direction A: Lamplit Ink (recommended)

### A-test-hub-evening (16:9)

Reference: Engine capture of the courtyard from its real camera (about 30° down, 31° field of view): new game, `go hub`, early evening. Paint over it, keeping its framing exactly.

```text
The courtyard at early evening, seen from above at the game's fixed angle. An enormous ancient tree leans about fifteen degrees out over three different pools: a wide sunlit one under the waterfall's spray, a deep dark one among the roots, a small mirror-still one ringed with flat stones that reflects the lit city above. Beside the tree a tall, lazy, pale duck-egg ribbon of waterfall drifts down the cliff. Four small houses stand around the tree: a tall, narrow grey stone house with a steep blue roof and a green door; a low timber lodge with a teal roof and a porch beside the pools; a low plaster shop with a violet roof, a chimney and a bowed window full of jars; and a round stone lodge with an ochre dome. Each has an old lamp over its door, dark and unlit. A few low iron lamp posts are just being lit. The canyon walls rise out of the top of the frame in stacked strata, stairs and warm lit windows. No people. The Atrium: the shaded canyon floor at the very bottom of Stairfall, a city built down a cliff face in tiers of stairs, arches and lit windows. Down here the light is the cool bright sky far overhead; warmth appears only where someone has carried a light down or lit one. Things here have arrived from above and settled: books in a pile, gold in the silt, a key in a reliquary. Palette: cistern slate, verdigris, deep-pool teal, canyon moss and fall-mist grey for the shade; parchment and sandstone for paper and stone; lamp amber, ember and a little Hem-clay red as rare warm accents. Quiet and unhurried. Gouache and ink, like a plate from an old naturalist's field book: observed, not whimsical. Flat opaque colour shapes with quiet visible brushwork, slightly dry edges and paper grain in the lights; a warm brown-black ink line, heavier on silhouettes and broken where light falls; two or three flat value steps per material, with coloured shadows of verdigris and slate; restrained and hand-made.
```
